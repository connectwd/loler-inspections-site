## Scope of this guide

This guide provides general educational information about lifting beams and LOLER thorough examinations. It does **not** confirm that LOLER Inspections inspects lifting beams. Contact us to discuss your equipment and confirm whether it is within our inspection scope.

## What is a lifting beam?

A lifting beam is a below-the-hook lifting device used to support and distribute a load between lifting points. Designs vary, so its configuration, markings and intended use matter when assessing suitability and examination requirements.

## When does a lifting beam require a LOLER thorough examination?

Where a lifting beam is lifting equipment within the scope of LOLER, it must be thoroughly examined at the applicable intervals or in accordance with a written scheme of examination. The correct arrangements depend on the equipment and how it is used.

> **Do not assume one inspection interval applies to every lifting beam.** The applicable category and interval must be established for the particular equipment and its use.

## What may an examination consider?

- Identification, safe working load and relevant markings.
- Visible deformation, cracking, corrosion or damage.
- Welds, lifting points, pins, connections and other load-bearing features.
- Whether the equipment remains suitable for its intended configuration and use.

## What happens if a defect is found?

The competent person records the examination findings and identifies defects that could present a risk. Statutory reporting and notification requirements apply in specified circumstances. Equipment should not be returned to use until it is safe to do so.

## How often should a lifting beam be examined?

LOLER provides default thorough-examination intervals for categories of lifting equipment, while a properly drawn-up written scheme can set the examination regime. The applicable category and interval must be confirmed for the particular equipment and its use.
