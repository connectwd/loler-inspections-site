LOLER equipment guide pages

A small, maintainable set of useful equipment guides, integrated into the existing Astro website without a CMS or paid service.

1. Objective

Add an equipment information area that can attract relevant search traffic, answer common questions about thorough examinations and give visitors a clear route to enquire.

  • Use one reusable page template rather than designing every page independently.
  • Start with a small number of equipment types approved by the business.
  • Publish distinct, useful information rather than near-identical keyword pages.
  • Use Astro static generation and Markdown. No database, CMS or runtime service is required.

Scope rule: a guide must not imply that LOLER Inspections provides inspections for an equipment type until the business confirms that service. General educational information and the company's actual service offering must be clearly distinguished.

LOLER inspections of lifting beams

Understand what lifting beams are used for, when they may require a thorough examination and the types of condition issues an examination may identify.

Make an enquiry

What is a lifting beam?

A lifting beam is a below-the-hook lifting device used to support and distribute a load between lifting points. Designs vary, so its configuration, markings and intended use matter when assessing suitability and examination requirements.

When does a lifting beam require a LOLER thorough examination?

Where a lifting beam is lifting equipment within the scope of LOLER, it must be thoroughly examined at the applicable intervals or in accordance with a written scheme of examination. The correct arrangements depend on the equipment and how it is used.

Do not assume one inspection interval applies to every lifting beam. The applicable category and interval must be established for the particular equipment and use.

What may an examination consider?

  • Identification, safe working load and relevant markings.
  • Visible deformation, cracking, corrosion or damage.
  • Welds, lifting points, pins, connections and other load-bearing features.
  • Whether the equipment remains suitable for its intended configuration and use.

What happens if a defect is found?

The competent person records the examination findings and identifies defects that could present a risk. Statutory reporting and notification requirements apply in specified circumstances. Equipment should not be returned to use until it is safe to do so.

How often should a lifting beam be examined?

LOLER provides default thorough-examination intervals for categories of lifting equipment, while a properly drawn-up written scheme can set the examination regime. The applicable category and interval must be confirmed for the particular equipment and its use.

5. Content standard for every guide

  1. Introduction: identify the equipment and explain what the guide covers.
  2. Equipment overview: explain purpose and common configurations.
  3. LOLER applicability: explain when it is likely to fall within the regulations, with relevant qualifications.
  4. Examination arrangements: explain default intervals and written schemes without overgeneralising.
  5. Examination scope: give equipment-specific examples of components and defects.
  6. Reports and defects: explain findings and next steps in plain English.
  7. Enquiry CTA: reflect the company's confirmed services.
  8. Sources: link to relevant HSE guidance and legislation.
